Romance fraud · 18+

Romance scams explained: the script, the first money ask, and what to do now

A calm hub — not a scare page. What romance fraud is, how dating scams usually unfold, the first money-ask patterns, and the exact moves if cash already left.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A romance scam is a fake relationship used to take money. Romance scammers build trust on a dating or social app, move you to WhatsApp or Telegram, then ask for gift cards, wires, crypto, or deposits on a fake trading site. Stop sending the moment money appears. If it already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3 (US). Longer flags: romance scam checklist.

Key takeaways

  • The romance is the product pitch. The steal is gift cards, wires, crypto, or a fake platform.
  • The first ask is designed to sound small and temporary. It rarely is.
  • One strong flag is enough — you do not need a perfect scorecard.
  • Primary moves: stop sending, call the bank, report.

What a romance scam is

The FTC and FBI use plain language: someone pretends to be a romantic partner so they can get money. In search and police reports you will also see romance fraud and dating scams. Same crime, different labels. A catfish who never asks for cash is a different problem. A bad date is a different problem. Romance scammers want a payment rail they can keep using.

US complaints keep climbing because the funnel is boring and reliable: polished profile → private chat → a crisis or an “opportunity.” Shame is part of the design. People wait. Waiting is how a $200 gift card becomes a five-figure wire. Online dating can still be ordinary if you keep a few rules — see is online dating safe.

How dating scams usually unfold

  1. Contact. Dating app, Instagram, Facebook, or a “wrong number” text. Photos are stolen or generated. The job story explains travel, odd hours, or why they cannot meet.
  2. Warmth. Daily check-ins. They remember your dog’s name. Love-bombing in a week still happens; slower crews now wait because everyone has read that tell.
  3. Off-app. WhatsApp, Telegram, or iMessage — “the app keeps logging me out.” The report button stays behind.
  4. First money ask. Small, urgent, emotional. Hospital, ticket, customs, “prove you care.” Details: romance scam scripts.
  5. Escalate or pivot. Repeat crises — or a trading screenshot and a fake platform. That second path is pig butchering.

You do not need every step. Plenty of people send on step four because the chat already felt like a relationship.

Common scripts and the first money ask

Romance scammers do not open with “wire me everything.” The first request is written to sound like a temporary favor between people who already care about each other:

  • Medical or accident crisis — hospital deposit, medicine, a child’s emergency. Urgency plus guilt.
  • Travel, visa, or customs hold — they were “coming to see you” until a fee appeared.
  • Military, oil, or overseas work — the job explains the distance and the sudden paperwork.
  • Gift cards “just this once” — irreversible on purpose. Not how real people pay rent.
  • A small crypto “test” — the hook before larger deposits or an unlock fee.

How to hear the first ask while the chat is still open: romance scam scripts — spotting the first request for money. You do not owe them a debate about the story.

Red flags

Use this as a walk-away list, not a quiz. One strong flag is enough to pause; two or more means stop.

  • Rush off the original app before live video or a real meet.
  • Refuse unscripted live video, or only send clips. How to check: video chat safety.
  • Any ask for cash, gift cards, wires, Zelle, or crypto from someone you have not met.
  • A trading platform or “unlock fee” — pig butchering, not a hot tip. Dating-app version: pig butchering on dating apps.
  • Isolation — “don’t tell your family, they won’t understand us.”
  • Story details that slip while the money pitch stays smooth.

The longer walk-away list: romance scam red flags checklist. Shorter overview: dating scam red flags.

You do not owe them a debate.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay on the app until live video” and “I do not send money to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

What to do now

Stop sending

No “one last” gift card. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Call the bank or card same day

Say romance scam or romance fraud — the wording helps the fraud team. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, and any crypto on-ramp. Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile and file with authorities

Block and report on the app. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Step-by-step: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts. Shame is part of the design; a trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under pressure.

Crisis asks vs pig butchering vs sextortion

Most romance fraud is a crisis script and an irreversible payment. Pig butchering is the long version: weeks of warmth, then a fake investment dashboard. A small withdrawal that “works” is the hook. Unlock fees are the second steal. Hub: pig butchering scams explained.

If they have intimate images and threaten to leak them, that is sextortion. Do not pay. Paying does not buy deletion. Save evidence and follow sextortion help. AI faces and polished English show up in all three — AI dating scams.

FAQ

What is a romance scam?

A fake relationship used to extract money — also called romance fraud or a dating scam. Operators build trust, move you off-app, then ask for gift cards, wires, crypto, or deposits on a site they control.

What do romance scammers usually ask for first?

Something small and urgent: a hospital bill, a ticket, customs fees, or a gift card “just this once.” Crypto tests and unlock fees come later. Full first-ask catalog: romance scam scripts.

What should I do if I already sent money?

Stop. Do not pay a release fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file with FTC ReportFraud and IC3 in the US (Action Fraud UK, Scamwatch AU). Checklist: report a dating scam.

Do romance scams only target older people?

No. Losses among older adults are well documented, but dating-app romance scams hit every adult age group. The script adapts; the money ask does not.

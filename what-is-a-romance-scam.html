Romance fraud · 18+

What is a romance scam? How it works and how to spot it early

A plain-language definition — not a scare page. What romance fraud is, the typical dating-app arc, the first money ask, and the early signs that mean walk away.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A romance scam is a fake relationship used to take money. Also called romance fraud or a dating scam. Operators build trust on a dating or social app, move you to WhatsApp or Telegram, then ask for gift cards, wires, crypto, or deposits on a fake trading site. Stop sending the moment money appears. Full hub: romance scams explained.

Key takeaways

  • The romance is the product pitch. The steal is gift cards, wires, crypto, or a fake platform.
  • The first ask is designed to sound small and temporary. It rarely is.
  • Spot it early: off-app rush, refuse live video, any money ask before you have met.
  • If cash left: stop, bank same day, report.

What a romance scam is

Searchers type what is a romance scam when a chat suddenly feels wrong — or after money already moved. The FTC and FBI use plain language: someone pretends to be a romantic partner so they can get paid. In police reports and news you will also see romance fraud and dating scams. Same crime, different labels.

A catfish who never asks for cash is a different problem. A bad date is a different problem. Romance scammers want a payment rail they can keep using — gift cards, wires, Zelle, crypto, or a fake investment dashboard. Broader types overview: online dating scams. Longer hub with scripts and flags: romance scams explained.

US complaints keep climbing because the funnel is boring and reliable. Shame is part of the design. People wait. Waiting is how a $200 gift card becomes a five-figure wire. Online dating can still be ordinary if you keep a few rules — see is online dating safe.

How a romance scam usually works

Most romance fraud follows the same arc. You do not need every step — plenty of people send on the first money ask because the chat already felt like a relationship.

  1. Contact. Dating app, Instagram, Facebook, or a “wrong number” text. Photos are stolen or AI-generated. The job story explains travel, odd hours, or why they cannot meet.
  2. Warmth. Daily check-ins. They remember your dog’s name. Love-bombing in a week still happens; slower crews now wait because everyone has read that tell.
  3. Off-app. WhatsApp, Telegram, or iMessage — “the app keeps logging me out.” The report button stays behind.
  4. First money ask. Small, urgent, emotional. Hospital, ticket, customs, “prove you care.”
  5. Escalate or pivot. Repeat crises — or a trading screenshot and a fake platform. That second path is pig butchering.

The first money ask

Romance scammers do not open with “wire me everything.” The first request is written to sound like a temporary favor between people who already care about each other:

  • Medical or accident crisis — hospital deposit, medicine, a child’s emergency. Urgency plus guilt.
  • Travel, visa, or customs hold — they were “coming to see you” until a fee appeared.
  • Military, oil, or overseas work — the job explains the distance and the sudden paperwork.
  • Gift cards “just this once” — irreversible on purpose. Not how real people pay rent.
  • A small crypto “test” — the hook before larger deposits or an unlock fee.

How to hear the first ask while the chat is still open: romance scam scripts — spotting the first request for money. You do not owe them a debate about the story.

How to spot it early

Use this as a walk-away list, not a quiz. One strong flag is enough to pause; two or more means stop.

  • Rush off the original app before live video or a real meet.
  • Refuse unscripted live video, or only send clips. Habits: video chat safety.
  • Any ask for cash, gift cards, wires, Zelle, or crypto from someone you have not met.
  • A trading platform or “unlock fee” — pig butchering, not a hot tip.
  • Isolation — “don’t tell your family, they won’t understand us.”
  • Story details that slip while the money pitch stays smooth.

Practical checklists: online dating red flags · romance scam red flags checklist.

You do not owe them a debate.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay on the app until live video” and “I do not send money to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

What to do now

Stop sending

No “one last” gift card. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Call the bank or card same day

Say romance scam or romance fraud — the wording helps the fraud team. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, and any crypto on-ramp. Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile and file with authorities

Block and report on the app. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Step-by-step: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts. Shame is part of the design; a trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under pressure.

FAQ

What is a romance scam?

A fake relationship used to extract money — also called romance fraud or a dating scam. Operators build trust, move you off-app, then ask for gift cards, wires, crypto, or deposits on a site they control. Hub: romance scams explained.

How does a romance scam usually work?

Polished profile → daily warmth → rush to WhatsApp or Telegram → small urgent money ask. Some crews later sell a fake trading platform (pig butchering). First-ask language: romance scam scripts.

What should I do if I think I am in a romance scam?

Stop. Do not pay a release fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file with FTC ReportFraud and IC3 in the US (Action Fraud UK, Scamwatch AU). Checklist: report a dating scam.

Is pig butchering the same as a romance scam?

Related, not identical. A simple romance scam often asks for gift cards or a wire for a crisis. Pig butchering uses weeks of warmth to sell a fake investment platform and large deposits. Explainer: pig butchering scams.

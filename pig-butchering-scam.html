Pig butchering scams explained: 12 red flags before you send money

Not a scare page — a hub. What the con is, the flags that show up before a deposit, and the exact moves if money already left. Dating-app angle covered in a sister guide.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A pig butchering scam fattens trust with romance or friendship, then steers you to a fake crypto or trading site. Small withdrawals that “work” are the hook. Stop depositing the moment returns, wallets, or unlock fees appear. If money already left: bank same day, then FTC and IC3 (US). Dating-specific arc: pig butchering on dating apps.

Key takeaways

  • The relationship is bait; the fake platform is the steal.
  • One strong money flag is enough — you do not need all twelve.
  • Unlock fees and “taxes to release funds” are the second steal.
  • Primary moves: stop depositing, call the bank, report.

What pig butchering is

The name translates a Mandarin phrase (sha zhu pan) for fattening a pig before slaughter. Operators invest weeks of attention — often starting on dating apps, LinkedIn, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — until you feel known. Then they introduce trading, a private fund, or a sleek app that looks like a cousin of Coinbase.

US, UK, and Australian agencies have described the same funnel for years: warm contact → private chat → fake investment dashboard → larger and larger deposits → frozen account or silence. Losses are often five or six figures because the romance made the charts feel personal.

How the funnel works

  1. Contact. Polished photos (stolen or AI), a job story that explains odd hours or travel, patient chat.
  2. Off-platform. WhatsApp, Telegram, or iMessage — “the app keeps logging me out.”
  3. Soft pitch. Screenshots of profits, a “friend’s platform,” an invite-only link you did not type yourself.
  4. Hook. A small deposit and a withdrawal that actually arrives. That success is engineered.
  5. Scale. Larger deposits. Then a tax, KYC unlock fee, or frozen balance. Then pressure or ghosting.

12 red flags before you send money

Use this as a walk-away list, not a quiz. One strong flag is enough to pause; two or more means stop.

  1. They steer every warm chat toward “investing together.” Romance that always ends in charts.
  2. Rush off the original app before live video or a real meet — classic handoff from dating or social apps.
  3. Refuse unscripted live video, or only send clips. Identity stays optional while money talk grows.
  4. A trading platform you did not find yourself — invite link, APK, or “exclusive” URL from chat.
  5. Guaranteed returns or “can’t lose” language sold as a private tip, not regulated advice.
  6. A small withdrawal that works perfectly right when you hesitate — the hook, not proof the site is real.
  7. Pressure to deposit more quickly — limited slots, “the window closes tonight,” guilt if you pause.
  8. Ask for crypto, gift cards, wires, or Zelle to fund the “account” — irreversible rails by design.
  9. Unlock fee, tax, or “release the funds” after a big balance appears on screen.
  10. Support only answers through your match — no independent phone number or regulated firm you can verify.
  11. Story details that slip — cities, ages, firm names — while the investment pitch stays smooth.
  12. Isolation — “don’t tell your bank / family, they won’t understand our opportunity.” Secrecy protects the con.

You do not owe them a debate about the chart.

Real partners and real brokers do not onboard you to a brokerage from a dating or WhatsApp chat. Walking away is the whole skill.

What to do now

Stop depositing

No “one last” transfer. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Call the bank or card same day

Report romance or investment scam. Flag wires, Zelle, and any crypto on-ramp (Coinbase, Cash App, etc.). Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile and file with authorities

Block and report on the app. US: FTC ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Step-by-step: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of chat, wallet addresses, URLs, and payment receipts. Shame is part of the design; a trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under pressure.

Vs. ordinary romance scams

A simple romance scam often wants gift cards or a wire for a hospital or travel crisis. Pig butchering wants large deposits into a site the crew controls. The warmth lasts longer because the payoff is larger. Both deserve the same first rule: never send money to someone you met online. Ordinary romance-fraud hub: romance scams explained. FAQ

What is a pig butchering scam?

A long con that builds romance or friendship, then moves you onto a fake investment or crypto platform. Early withdrawals may work so you deposit more. Then the balance freezes, fees appear, or the site vanishes.

What should I do if I already sent money?

Stop depositing. Do not pay an unlock fee. Call your bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file with FTC and IC3 in the US (Action Fraud UK, Scamwatch AU). Full checklist: report a dating scam.

Does this only happen on dating apps?

No. Dating apps are common entry points, but LinkedIn, Instagram, Telegram, and “wrong number” texts are used too. The dating-app version is covered in pig butchering on dating apps.

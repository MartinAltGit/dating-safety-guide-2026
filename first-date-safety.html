First date safety: the boring rules still beat a clever app

Verification badges do not walk you to your car. Do live video on the app, meet in public, bring your own way home, and tell a person who likes you where you are.

Updated Aug 17, 202618+ · independent

Most first dates are awkward and fine. The checklist exists for the ones that are not, and for the match who has been perfect in chat because they were never going to show up as themselves.

Before you agree to meet

  • Live video on the app. Not a clip. A two-minute call with a spontaneous ask. If they refuse for a week, there is no date.
  • Keep the thread on the app until you have met. Phone numbers are a gift you can give later.
  • Pick a public place you already know. Daylight if you can. A lobby bar, a busy café. Not their apartment, not a parked car, not a hike on a first meet.
  • Your own transport. Do not get in their car. Do not let them “just drop you.”
  • Tell a friend the plan: name they’re using, app, time, place, when you’ll text after. Share live location if that’s your habit.
  • Leave the spare key and the “I live at…” details out of chat. Same for kids’ schools and regular gyms — those photos leak routines.

During

Sit where you can leave. Pay for your own drink if that makes it easier to walk. If your gut turns, you do not need a story. “I’m heading out” is a complete sentence. Apps have a panic or report flow; screenshot the profile before you go in case they vanish.

After

Text the friend you briefed. If something felt off, report in-app while the chat still exists. If they push to come over the same night, that’s not romance, that’s pressure. Red flags still apply in person: money, isolation, rush.

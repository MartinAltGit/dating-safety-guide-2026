Romance scam scripts: how to spot the first request for money

The first ask is written to sound like a favor, not a crime. Here is how those scripts sound, why they work, and what to do before a card number leaves your hand.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

The first request is usually small, urgent, and hard to reverse. Hospital, ticket, customs, gift cards, or a crypto “test” — from someone you have not met, after they pushed you off the dating app. Amount is not the test. Stop, do not negotiate the story, and if anything already left, call the bank. Hub: romance scams explained.

Key takeaways

  • First asks are written as temporary favors between people who already “care.”
  • Gift cards, wires, and crypto are the tell — not the size of the number.
  • Guilt and secrecy (“don’t tell anyone”) protect the script.
  • You can refuse in one sentence. Then report.

How to hear the first ask

Stack these. One is a pause. Two or more is a walk-away:

  • Timing. The ask arrives after warmth — sometimes days, sometimes weeks — not in the first “hey.”
  • Channel. WhatsApp, Telegram, or iMessage. The dating app’s report button is already behind you.
  • Rail. Gift cards, prepaid cards, wires, Zelle, Cash App, Western Union, or a wallet address. Irreversible by design.
  • Urgency plus secrecy. Tonight. Don’t tell your sister. They won’t understand us.
  • No shared reality. You have not met. Live video was refused, delayed, or replaced with a clip. See video chat safety.

If those line up, the story can be fluent and still be a script. Grammar got better. The payment method did not.

Common first-ask scripts

Each block is the shape of the line, why it works, and the tell. You do not need a word-for-word match.

Hospital, accident, or “I need medicine tonight”

They or a relative are in an ER that will not treat them without a deposit. Photos of a wristband or a bill may follow. Why it works: decent people do not want someone to suffer. The tell: a stranger you have not met needs your irreversible payment in the next hour, and they already know which store sells the cards.

Stranded travel — ticket, hotel, or “I was coming to you”

A flight was cancelled. A passport is held. They were finally going to visit. Why it works: the romance now has a date on a calendar. The tell: airlines, hotels, and embassies do not get paid in iTunes or Google Play cards. Real travel problems have receipts you can verify without sending money to a chat handle.

Customs, visa, lawyer, or “they will deport me”

A fee appeared at a border, a lawyer needs a retainer, a package of “gifts for you” is held. Why it works: bureaucracy feels plausible if you have never filed those forms. The tell: government fees are not paid through a dating match’s wallet. Pressure to stay quiet is the product.

Military leave, oil rig, or overseas contract

The job explains why they cannot FaceTime and why paperwork suddenly costs money — leave papers, equipment, a replacement. Why it works: distance was already in the story. The tell: US service members and real contractors do not fund official process through a civilian’s gift cards. Odd hours plus a money ask is a stacked flag, not proof of the job.

Gift cards “just this once”

They cannot use a bank. Their account is frozen. Cards are “safer.” Sometimes they offer to pay you back next week. Why it works: the number looks small and temporary. The tell: gift cards are cash that cannot be clawed back. That is why romance fraud uses them. A small card is not a trial of your character. It is a test of whether the rail works.

Crypto “just try a little so we can invest together”

A screenshot of profits. A “friend’s platform.” Buy $200 of Bitcoin first. A withdrawal that actually arrives. Why it works: success on a small amount feels like evidence. The tell: that success is the hook. Larger deposits and an unlock fee come next. That path has its own hub: pig butchering scams and the dating-app arc.

“Prove you care” / relationship test

If you loved them you would help. After everything they shared. They thought you were different. Why it works: it attacks your self-image, not your spreadsheet. The tell: a real partner does not invoice affection. Guilt after you hesitate is data.

Unlock fee, tax, or “release the funds” (usually the second steal)

This is rarely the first ask. It shows up after a dashboard already shows a big balance. The tell: more money will not unlock earlier money. Stop. Call the bank. Do not pay the fee.

You do not have to disprove the hospital.

Your job is not forensic debate. Your job is “I don’t send money to people I haven’t met.” If they are real and in crisis, they have family, employers, and institutions that are not a dating chat.

What real money talk looks like

Adults who actually date sometimes split a dinner they both attended. They do not request gift cards from a stranger on Telegram. They can do live video. They can wait a week. They do not punish you for telling a friend. If the conversation only works when you stay silent and pay, it is not a relationship. Longer flags: romance scam checklist and dating scam red flags.

What to do at the first ask

Refuse in one sentence

Do not workshop their story. Do not send a smaller amount “as a compromise.” Close the payment path.

Save the ask, then report

Screenshot the message, the handle, and any wallet or card instructions. Report and block on the original app. Full path: how to report a dating scam.

If anything already left

Call the bank or card the same day. US: FTC ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. AU: Scamwatch. Do not pay an unlock fee.

Tell someone you trust

The next script is often “they didn’t understand, please help again.” A friend who already knows makes that harder.

FAQ

How do romance scammers first ask for money?

Small, urgent, emotional — hospital, travel, customs, or a gift card “just this once.” Crypto tests come after. The ask follows warmth and an off-app move, from someone you have not met.

Is a small gift card request still a romance scam?

If it comes from someone you met online and have not verified in person, treat it as one. Amount is not the test. Irreversible rails plus a crisis story are.

What should I say when they ask?

“I don’t send money to people I haven’t met” is enough. Then stop. You do not owe a closing argument. If money already left, follow the report steps the same day.

What if they already sent me a little money?

A small transfer to you can be a hook — especially before they ask you to “invest” or refund a “mistake.” Do not send it back through a new rail they control. Talk to your bank.

Dating App Pricing: What You'll Actually Pay in 2026

The price on the signup screen is rarely what you end up paying. Dating apps sell you on a cheap first month, then quietly convert to longer, more expensive cycles after the trial. Here is how the billing really works, so you are not surprised at renewal.

How the billing math works

  1. Intro rates step up — the first 1–3 months are discounted, then the plan jumps to the standard rate automatically.
  2. Long billing cycles — many apps push 3, 6, or 12-month plans that renew all at once. A "small" weekly charge can become a big 6-month charge.
  3. Auto-renew is on by default — unless you turn it off before the renewal date, it keeps charging on the same card.
  4. Trials convert silently — a 7-day free trial flips into a paid plan unless you cancel 2 days before it ends.

Before you pay, ask three questions

  1. What is the price after the intro period, and when does it start?
  2. How long is the renewal cycle — and how much is the full charge?
  3. Where do I cancel (app store settings vs. in-app), and do I get a confirmation?

How to protect yourself

  • Set a calendar reminder for 2 days before every trial or renewal date.
  • Cancel through the store subscription settings, not by deleting the app.
  • Keep the cancellation confirmation email as proof.

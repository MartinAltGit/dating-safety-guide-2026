Romance scam red flags checklist: the calm walk-away list

Not a panic sheet. A handbook you can skim while the chat is still open — WhatsApp rushes, gift cards, crypto, and every other tell that money is next.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Stop sending money the moment any of these show up. One strong flag is enough to pause. Two or more means walk away, report the profile, and — if cash already left — call the bank the same day. Shorter overview: dating scam red flags.

Key takeaways

  • Off-app chat in the first days is usually about leaving the report button behind.
  • Gift cards, wires, crypto, and “unlock” fees are never a relationship test.
  • Refuse live video, or only send a pre-recorded clip? Treat it as not them.
  • Primary move: stop sending and report. Everything else is secondary.

Romance scams still start on dating apps and social apps. The script has gotten smoother — better grammar, AI faces, slower “I love you” — but the money ask has not changed. Use this checklist as a slow read, not a quiz. You do not need every box checked.

Chat and contact flags

  • Rush to WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, or iMessage — “the app is glitchy,” “I get logged out,” or “I prefer private.” Dating apps keep a report trail. Private apps do not. Stay on-platform until you have done live video.
  • They refuse to stay on the app after you say no — polite pushback is data. Persistent push is the script.
  • New number or handle every few days — burned accounts get replaced. Ask why the old one vanished.
  • They message from multiple apps at once — dating app plus Instagram plus WhatsApp, all before you have met. Volume is not intimacy.
  • Odd hours that always match “oil rig / military / overseas trading” — the job story explains why they never meet and why money is urgent later.

Money and payment flags

  • Any ask for cash, gift cards, or prepaid cards — travel, hospital, visa, customs, “prove you care.” Gift cards are irreversible; that is why scammers like them.
  • Crypto wallet, seed phrase, or “just buy a little Bitcoin first” — not a relationship. Not a test. Stop.
  • Investment platform, trading screenshot, or “my friend runs a private fund” — that is pig butchering, not a hot tip.
  • A small withdrawal that “works” — the hook. Larger deposits come next; then a tax, unlock fee, or frozen account.
  • Unlock fee, tax, or “release the funds” — the second steal. Do not pay it.
  • Wire, Zelle, Cash App, Western Union to a stranger — treat as gone once sent. Call the bank the same day anyway.
  • Link to “verify” on another site or a fake exclusive app — card-skimming pages dressed as romance.

Identity and video flags

  • Refuse live video, or “camera is broken” for a week — a gym clip or TikTok is not a call.
  • Live video that feels scripted or delayed — deepfake pipelines exist. Ask for something spontaneous: wave a hand, turn 90°, hold up three fingers, say a word you invent.
  • Blank reverse image search — stolen photos still light up; AI faces often do not. A blank result is inconclusive, not a green light. See also fake profiles and AI dating scams.
  • Too-perfect modeling photos, no candid shots — not proof alone, but stack it with other flags.
  • They will only voice-call, never show a face — voice can be cloned or borrowed. Face live, on the dating app, is the bar.

Story and pressure flags

  • Love bombing inside a week — soulmate language before shared reality. Still a script when the grammar is perfect.
  • Crisis that only money solves — medical emergency, stranded abroad, customs hold, child’s tuition. Urgency is the product.
  • Story details that slip — city names, ages, kids, ship names. Write them down once; ask again later.
  • Isolation ask — “don’t tell your friends, they won’t understand us.” Shame and secrecy protect the con.
  • Guilt when you hesitate — “I thought you were different,” “after all we’ve shared.” You are allowed to slow down.
  • They never can meet in person, for months — distance happens; permanent impossibility plus money ask is the pattern.

You do not owe them a debate.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay on the app until live video.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

What to do instead

Stop sending

Primary rule. No gift card “one last time.” No unlock fee. Money already gone is painful; more money does not buy it back.

Save evidence, then report

Screenshots of profile, chat, payment asks, wallet addresses. Report and block in the app. Full path: how to report a dating scam.

Call the bank same day

Card, wire, or on-ramp (Coinbase, Cash App). Say romance or investment scam if that is what it was. US: FTC ReportFraud + IC3. UK: Action Fraud. AU: Scamwatch.

Tell someone you trust

Shame is part of the design. A friend who knows reduces the chance you send again under pressure.

FAQ

What are the biggest romance scam red flags?

Rush to WhatsApp or Telegram, money / gift cards / crypto, refuse live video, love bombing, and any investment or unlock-fee pitch. Stack them; do not wait for every flag.

Is moving to WhatsApp always a scam?

Not always — but it is the most common next step in romance and pig-butchering cons. Stay on the dating app until you have done an unscripted live video call. If they push after you say no, that is data.

What if I already sent money?

Stop. Do not pay a release fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file with authorities. Step-by-step: report a dating scam.

What is catfishing? Fake identity online, and how to spot it early

A plain-language definition — not a scare page. What catfishing is, how it differs from romance scams and pig butchering, the live-video check, and what to do if money or ID comes up.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Catfishing is using a fake identity so someone will trust them. Stolen photos, an AI face, a name and job that are not theirs. It is not always a money crime. When the fake person asks for gift cards, wires, crypto, or copies of your ID, treat it as a catfishing scam. Online dating can still be ordinary if you keep a few rules — see is online dating safe.

Key takeaways

  • Catfishing is the fake identity. A romance scam is the fake relationship used to take money. They overlap; they are not the same word.
  • Live, unscripted video on the original app is the check. Clips and a blank reverse-image search are not.
  • A gift-card, wire, or “send your ID to verify” ask means stop. Money details: catfishing scam.
  • If anything already left: stop, keep evidence, report.

What catfishing is

Searchers type what is catfishing when a match will not show their face on a live call — or after a photo they trusted turns out to belong to someone else. The word is older than dating apps. It means building a false identity so another person will stay in the chat: borrowed pictures, a job story, a city, sometimes a whole family. The person on the other side is real. The profile is not.

That is different from a bad photo, a slightly older age, or someone who is shy on camera for a week. Catfishing is a sustained lie about who they are. Motive varies. Some people want attention or a relationship they think their real face would not get. Some crews use the same fake face to run money asks on dozens of matches. The photos do not tell you which one you have. The payment ask does.

US agencies usually file the money version under romance fraud or identity theft, not under the TV word. The labels still matter for search: people say catfish when they mean “this person is not who the pictures say.” Types overview: online dating scams.

How catfishing differs from romance scams and pig butchering

Keep the three ideas separate so you know which page to read next.

  • Catfishing — fake identity. The question is “Are they the person in the photos?” Money may never appear. This page.
  • Romance scam / romance fraud — fake relationship used to extract money. Operators often catfish first, then ask for a hospital bill, a ticket, or gift cards. Hub: romance scams explained.
  • Pig butchering — long warmth, then a fake trading or crypto platform and large deposits. The romance is inventory; the wallet is the product. Hub: pig butchering scams. Dating-app arc: pig butchering on dating apps.

A catfish who never asks for cash is still a betrayal. It is not the same crime as a wire. Do not wait for a perfect label before you walk away from a refused live video. Practical walk-away list: online dating red flags.

Common patterns on dating apps and social

On Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, and similar apps the script is boring: polished photos, a job that explains travel or night shifts, daily warmth, then a push to WhatsApp, Telegram, or iMessage — “the app keeps logging me out.” The report button stays behind. Instagram, Facebook, and “wrong number” texts use the same photos and the same off-platform rush.

  • Stolen or generated photos — a model, a soldier, a nurse, an oil-rig worker. Reverse image search still catches stolen shots. AI faces often return nothing. That blank result is not a pass. How to look: how to spot fake profiles.
  • A thin or recycled bio — few friends, new account, details that slip (city, age, the name of the dog) while the affection stays smooth.
  • Always-on chat, never a real calendar — they can text at 2 a.m. They cannot pick a Tuesday for coffee or a five-minute live call.
  • Isolation — “don’t tell your friends yet, they won’t understand us.” Secrecy protects the lie, not the relationship.

AI faces and polished English show up here and in money scams. Separate guide: AI dating scams in 2026.

Identity verification: live video

Unscripted live video on the original app is the check that still works. A gym clip, a voice note, a “my camera is broken,” or a FaceTime that never quite connects is not a check. Ask for something they cannot have pre-recorded: wave a hand across the face, turn 90°, hold up three fingers, read a word you invent. If they stall, offer a new time once. If they stall again, you have your answer.

Stay on the dating app for that call when you can. Off-app video is easier to fake with a deepfake overlay or a second person holding the phone. Habits and recording risk: video chat safety.

You do not owe them a debate about the camera.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay on the app until live video” and “I do not send money or copies of my ID to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

When money or gift cards appear

The identity lie and the payment ask are different moments. The ask is designed to sound small and temporary: rent, a hospital deposit, a ticket “so I can come see you,” customs, a gift card “just this once.” Gift cards and crypto are irreversible on purpose. A request for your driver’s license, a selfie with a handwritten note, or a bank screenshot “to verify you” is identity theft, not romance.

That is the scam companion — first money-ask patterns, identity-theft angles, stop / bank / report: catfishing scam. Same rails as other dating fraud. Broader hub: online dating scams.

What to do

Stop sending

No “one last” gift card. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. No photo of your ID “just to prove you’re real.” More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Preserve evidence, then report

Screenshot the profile URL or username, photos, the chat, wallet addresses, and receipts before you block. Then report and block in the app so other people stop matching them. Step-by-step: how to report a dating scam.

If cash or data already left

Call the bank or card the same day. Say romance scam, catfishing scam, or identity theft — the wording helps the fraud team. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber.

Tell someone you trust

Shame is part of the design. A trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under pressure. You were targeted. That is not the same as being foolish.

FAQ

What is catfishing?

Using a fake identity online — stolen or AI-generated photos, a borrowed name — so someone will trust them. It is not always about money. When gift cards, wires, crypto, or copies of your ID appear, treat it as a catfishing scam.

How is catfishing different from a romance scam?

Catfishing is the fake identity. A romance scam is a fake relationship used to extract money. Many romance scammers catfish first. A catfish who never asks for cash is a different problem. Hub: romance scams explained. The investment version is pig butchering.

How do I know if I am being catfished?

Unscripted live video on the original app, with a spontaneous physical ask. Clips, voice notes, and a blank reverse-image search are not proof. Photo checks: spot fake profiles. Video habits: video chat safety.

What should I do if I think I am being catfished?

Stop sending money or documents. Save screenshots, report and block in the app, then file with FTC ReportFraud and IC3 in the US (Action Fraud UK, Scamwatch AU). Checklist: report a dating scam.

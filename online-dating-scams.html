Online dating scams: types, warning signs, and what to do

A calm map of how dating app scams usually work — romance fraud, pig butchering, and catfish money asks — plus the same first moves if cash already left.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Online dating scams use a fake connection to get money. The common shapes are romance scams (crisis gift cards and wires), pig butchering (warmth then a fake trading site), and catfish money asks (stolen face, faster cash pitch). Stop sending the moment money appears. If it already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3 (US). Deep dive: romance scams · pig butchering · how to report.

Key takeaways

  • The romance (or friendship) is the product pitch. The steal is gift cards, wires, crypto, or a fake platform.
  • Off-app chat plus a money ask is the core pattern across types.
  • One strong flag is enough — you do not need every step of the script.
  • Primary moves: stop sending, call the bank, report.

What online dating scams are

People search online dating scams and dating scams after a weird chat — or after money already moved. The FTC and FBI describe the same crime in plain language: someone pretends to be a romantic partner (or a fast friend) so they can get paid. Labels vary — romance fraud, dating app scams, catfish — but the payment rails stay familiar: gift cards, wires, Zelle, crypto, and fake investment dashboards.

Millions still date online without drama. The bad funnels are predictable. Safety habits that shrink risk: is online dating safe.

Main types

Romance scams

A fake relationship built on a dating or social app, then moved to WhatsApp or Telegram. The first money ask is usually small and urgent — hospital, stranded travel, customs, “just this once” gift cards. Later asks escalate. Full hub: romance scams explained. First-ask language: romance scam scripts.

Pig butchering (romance + fake investment)

Weeks of warmth, then a trading screenshot and a platform the operator controls. A small withdrawal that “works” is the hook. Unlock fees and taxes to “release” funds are the second steal. Hub: pig butchering scams explained. Dating-app arc: pig butchering on dating apps.

Catfish money asks

Stolen or generated photos, a thin backstory, and a faster path to cash — rent, tickets, a “family emergency” — without the long investment theater. The identity is fake; the payment ask is the point. Spotting fakes: how to spot fake profiles. AI faces: AI dating scams in 2026.

Shared warning signs

Across types, the early tells look alike. Use this as a walk-away list, not a quiz:

  • Rush off the original app before live video or a real meet.
  • Refuse unscripted live video, or only send clips.
  • Any ask for cash, gift cards, wires, or crypto from someone you have not met.
  • A trading platform or unlock fee after weeks of chat.
  • Love bombing paired with a crisis only money solves.
  • Isolation — “don’t tell your family.”

You do not owe them a debate.

Real people can handle “I stay on the app until live video” and “I do not send money to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

What to do now

Stop sending

No “one last” gift card. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Call the bank or card same day

Say romance scam, dating scam, or romance fraud — the wording helps the fraud team. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, and any crypto on-ramp.

Report the profile and file with authorities

Block and report on the app. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Step-by-step: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts. Shame is part of the design; a trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under pressure.

FAQ

What are the main types of online dating scams?

Romance scams (fake relationship, then gift cards or wires), pig butchering (long warmth then a fake investment platform), and catfish money asks (stolen or fake identity used for a faster cash pitch). AI faces show up across all three.

How do I spot an online dating scam early?

Rush off-app, refuse live video, love bombing, and any money or crypto ask. One strong flag is enough. Checklist: dating red flags.

What should I do if I already sent money?

Stop. Do not pay a release fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file with FTC ReportFraud and IC3 in the US. Full steps: how to report a dating scam.

Is every fake profile a dating scam?

No. A catfish who never asks for cash is a different problem. Romance and dating scams are defined by the money ask — or by the fake trading site that comes after the romance.

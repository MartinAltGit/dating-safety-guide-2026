How to get money back from a scam after a romance or dating con

A calm handbook for the day cash already left — not a promise you will be made whole. What bank and card disputes can still do, why crypto and gift cards are usually gone, and how to refuse the second-stage “recovery” pitch.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 9, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Getting money back from a scam depends on the rail, not on how real the chat felt. Call the bank or card the same day and say romance fraud. Cards and some transfers have a dispute window. Crypto and gift-card codes are often gone the hour they move. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Anyone who offers scam recovery for a fee is running a second steal. Hub: romance scams explained. Key takeaways

  • Stop sending. A “last ticket,” unlock fee, or tax does not buy the earlier money back.
  • Bank or card the same day beats a perfect report filed next week.

What “getting the money back” actually means

Romance and dating scams are built so the payment is hard to reverse. The person you were talking to is usually a script, not a partner in distress. The steal is gift cards, wires, payment apps, or crypto — sometimes after weeks of warmth, sometimes after a fake deployment, sometimes after a fake trading dashboard.

This page is scam recovery in the ordinary sense: what your bank, card, and a government report can still do. It is not a dating-app subscription refund. Unused Tinder or Bumble time is a different problem — app refunds.

Be honest with yourself about the rail. A same-day card dispute is a real process. A stranger on Telegram who “specializes in blockchain recovery” is not. Types of the first con: online dating scams.

Same-day steps

Order matters more than eloquence. The fraud desk can sometimes freeze a transfer that is still sitting. A polished write-up tomorrow does not help a wire that already cleared.

Stop sending

No last gift card. No customs hold. No tax to release a frozen “profit.” No photo of your ID “so they can refund you.” More money does not unlock earlier money. Do not send one last message to catch them. They already know.

Contact the bank or card the same day

Call the number on the back of the card or the bank’s fraud line — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Ask for a dispute, recall, or unauthorized-payment review. Flag wires, Zelle, Cash App, Apple Pay, PayPal, gift-card purchase points, and any crypto on-ramp.

Collect evidence before the chat vanishes

Screenshot the profile, the money ask, wallet addresses, URLs, usernames, phone numbers, and receipts. Copy the thread into a note. If they sent a link, paste it — do not open it again.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site so other people stop matching them. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile patterns; they are not a refund desk. Steps: how to report a dating scam.

Bank, card, crypto, gift cards

Tell the fraud team what it was. “I authorized a friend” and “it was a romance scam” are different scripts. Use the second if that is what happened.

  • Credit or debit card. Chargebacks and billing-error claims exist. Speed and a clear fraud story help. Keep the merchant name, amount, and date. Unauthorized or scam-induced charges belong on the issuer’s fraud line, not in a chat with the scammer.
  • Bank transfer or wire. Ask for a recall the same day. Once the receiving bank has paid out, the window is often closed. Same-day is the whole strategy.
  • Zelle, Cash App, Venmo, Apple Pay, PayPal. Open a fraud claim in the app and call the linked bank if a debit card funded it. “Friends and family” and instant rails are designed to be final. Still file. Do not send a “correction” payment.
  • Cryptocurrency. Once it leaves the exchange for a wallet you do not control, it is usually gone. Call the exchange’s fraud team immediately with the txid and destination address. Report those strings on IC3. Do not send more coins to “unlock” a frozen balance — that is the second bite of pig butchering.
  • Gift cards. Treat the code as cash the moment you shared it. Take the receipt back to the store the same day; sometimes an unused balance can be frozen. Do not buy a second card because someone offered to “trace” the first.

None of this is a guarantee. It is the honest map. Shame is part of the design; people wait because the chat “felt real.” Waiting is how a $200 card becomes a wire you cannot recall.

Evidence to keep

You are building a file for the bank and for a government report, not a case to argue with the operator.

  • Profile URL, username, photos, and the bio as it looked that day.
  • The chat — especially the first money ask and any “I love you, just this once.”
  • Payment confirmations, last-four of the card, wire references, gift-card barcodes, crypto txids and wallet addresses.
  • Phone numbers, WhatsApp or Telegram handles, email addresses, and any URL they sent.
  • Your own timeline: when you matched, when you left the app, when money left.

If the pressure included intimate images or a leak threat, that is sextortion. Do not pay. Save the threat, then use the sextortion help playbook alongside this one.

FTC ReportFraud, IC3, and the platform

Government reports rarely cut a check. They are still worth the hour. Clusters of the same wallet, number, or “deployed soldier” script are how cases get built. File even if you think the amount is small.

  • US — FTC: ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Romance scam or impersonation. Upload screenshots if the form allows. Save the confirmation number for the bank.
  • US — FBI IC3: IC3.gov. Use this for wires, crypto, and any internet-enabled fraud. Paste wallet addresses and handles exactly.
  • UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch and ReportCyber.
  • The app or social site: Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Match, Facebook, Instagram — report and block. That is how the account leaves other people’s stacks. In-app taps: how to report a dating scam.

If someone impersonated a US service member, you can also tip Army CID at cid.army.mil. That is a report, not a refund. Pattern: military romance scams.

A report is not a recovery contract.

The FTC and FBI do not take a cut, do not call you from a random cell, and do not ask you to buy gift cards or crypto so they can “process a return.” If that happens, it is a recovery scam. Hang up. Look up the agency yourself.

Recovery scams — the second steal

After a romance loss, a second crew often arrives. They buy lists of people who already paid. They pose as lawyers, blockchain forensics firms, consumer advocates, or even the FTC. They say the money is located. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or your login “to deposit the refund.”

The FTC’s August 2026 alert is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial information in exchange for help getting a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer.

  • They found you. You did not seek them out through a number you looked up yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “anti-money-laundering clearance.”
  • They want a remote-access app or your bank, exchange, or wallet login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to your bank or a friend because it would “tip off the original scammer.”

Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume. Paying them does not recover the first loss. It funds the second. There is no private firm that can reverse a blockchain transfer you already signed, and no official who needs iTunes cards to release a seizure.

If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report, and tell the first fraud agent there is a second charge. Do not hire a third person to fix the second.

FAQ

How do I get money back from a scam?

Stop sending. Call the bank or card the same day and say romance fraud. Save the chat, receipts, and wallet addresses. Report the profile, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3 (Action Fraud UK, Scamwatch AU). Cards and some transfers have a window. Crypto and gift cards are often gone. Checklist: report a dating scam.

Can I get money back if I sent crypto or gift cards?

Usually not. Crypto that left an exchange and gift-card codes you already shared are treated as final. Still call the exchange or the store the same day and put the txid or barcode in an IC3 report. Do not pay a tracer. Investment-romance version: pig butchering.

What is a recovery scam?

A second-stage fraud aimed at people who already lost money. A “recovery” lawyer, crypto firm, or fake official offers to get the funds back for a fee. The FTC: nobody legitimate charges you — or asks for bank details — to process a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. If images were used as leverage, see sextortion help.

Should I pay a fee to recover scam money?

No. A retainer, tax, or unlock charge is the second steal. Real recovery, when it happens, is your bank, card issuer, or payment app — not a stranger who found you after the first loss. Pattern of the first con: romance scams · online dating scams.

AI dating scams in 2026: the reverse image search is no longer enough

Stolen photos still show up on Google Images. Generated faces often don’t. Deepfake clips can survive a short call. The habits that still work are slower, and they are boring on purpose.

Date Safely editors Updated Aug 17, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Reverse image search catches stolen photos, not generated faces. Stay on the dating app. Ask for a live call with a physical gag — wave a hand across the face. Never send money or tap a “verify” link off the app.

Key takeaways

  • A blank reverse search means unknown, not safe.
  • A pre-recorded clip is not a live call.
  • Chatbots reply at all hours and dodge specifics.
  • Dating apps do not send you to random domains to verify a crush.

Norton’s 2026 intimacy report found that nearly half of current US online daters said they had been targeted by a dating scam, and most of those who were targeted said they fell for at least part of it. ESET’s 2026 dating-safety writeup makes the same shift: the old advice to “just reverse image search” still helps against stolen photos and does almost nothing against a face that never existed.

Four scams that actually show up

Generated profile photos

The face is attractive, consistent, and slightly too even. Teeth, earrings, and backgrounds look like a catalog. Reverse search is blank because there is no original. That blank search used to feel like a pass. Treat it as “unknown.”

Deepfake or face-swap video

A short clip of them “at the gym” or “in the hotel” is not a live call. Even a live call can be swapped for a minute. Security writers (ESET, The Conversation) keep repeating the same physical tests because the tools still fail them: wave a hand across the face, turn 90°, hold up a random object you name. Ask for two short calls on different days. Scripted one-offs are cheap. Repeat live calls are not.

Chatbot romance

The match is available at 3 a.m. and noon. They mirror your hobbies with eerie speed. Grammar is cleaner than any human texting in bed. They dodge specifics — employer, neighborhood, the name of the café they “just left.” Then they want WhatsApp “because the app is glitchy.”

Fake verification pages

A link to “confirm you’re real” or join an “invite-only” app. One in three people in a McAfee survey said they had been invited to an exclusive dating app; some typed payment details. Type URLs yourself. Dating apps do not send you to random domains to verify a crush.

What still works

Stay on the app until live video

Reports, IP mismatch tools, and bans exist there. WhatsApp and Telegram do not owe you that. A rush off-app is still the loudest tell in 2026.

Live, unscripted video — with a stupid request

Not a selfie video they already have. “Hold up the book next to you” or “wave across your face.” If they stall, you have your answer.

Look for a life outside the app

Old Instagram with tagged photos is a better signal than a two-week account with six grid shots. Recently created LinkedIn with no coworkers is a burner.

Never send money, crypto, or nudes to someone you have not met

Sextortion now uses generated intimate images. Investment pitches are pig butchering. There is no emergency that requires your wallet.

FAQ

Does photo verification on Tinder or Hinge mean they’re real?

It means a face matched a selfie liveness check at some point. It does not mean the person chatting with you is that face, or that they are not running a long con. Verification is useful. It is not a background check.

They sent a voice note. Is that enough?

Voice clones exist. A voice note is easier to fake than a live call with a physical gag. Ask for the live call anyway.
